Sub-issue of #58. The last two implementation steps of #58 are "create incident severity, ownership, escalation,
communication, evidence preservation, recovery and post-incident review procedures" and "run game-day scenarios for
database slowdown, object-storage outage, provider outage, stuck outbox, duplicate callback and failed deployment", and
its third verification line is "incident game-day and alert/runbook effectiveness review". Half of the first step is
already written and must not be rewritten: `docs/nfr/security-operations-targets.md` section 6 fixes the severity
definitions (6.1), the per-severity detect/acknowledge/mitigate/resolve/communicate targets (6.2), the lifecycle diagram
(6.3) and the security-specific immediate actions (6.4). What does not exist is the **procedure** — there is no
`docs/process/incident-response.md`, no incident record template, no communication template, no post-incident review
template and no game-day programme. `ls docs/process` returns nine files and none of them is an incident document; the
only template in `docs/templates/` is `threat-model.md`. Plan Section 6.4 makes this a gate: "E14 | Security/
performance/resilience/incident simulations pass | #56b, #58 game days".

**This issue writes the procedure, the templates and the scripts. It does not run the game days.** Running them is
**child 9b**, and the separation is deliberate rather than tidy: a game day injects a fault into a running stack, is
*observed* by a person who did not write the procedure, and produces a scored record of what the **alerts and runbooks
actually did**. That needs a Docker daemon, a live Alertmanager, and a second human — none of which a coding session
has, and `./scripts/dev doctor` in the planning container reports no Docker daemon at all. An issue whose acceptance
criteria cannot be met from a clean checkout is not one session; it is a session plus an exercise, and merging them
would produce either a blocked pull request or a fabricated drill record. Child 9b is carved out as an **exercise and
evidence** issue and says so in its own title.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/process/incident-response.md` is complete enough that a responder who has never seen it can follow it during
      a P1 without asking a question: every step names the document, runbook or endpoint it uses, and the
      evidence-preservation checklist is ordered and precedes every repair step.
- [ ] Every severity, target and lifecycle statement in the new documents is a **reference** to
      `docs/nfr/security-operations-targets.md` section 6, not a copy. A test or a review check confirms no number is
      duplicated, because two copies of a target will disagree within a release.
- [ ] Each of the six scripts is **executable by someone who did not write it** without asking a question: it names the
      environment, the exact command or container action that injects the fault, the command that reverses it, the alert
      expected and its section 6.2 detection target, the runbook to follow by filename, and what counts as a **failure
      of the exercise** as distinct from a failure of the system. A script that says "simulate a database slowdown" is
      not done.
- [ ] **Negative — the procedure makes an honest failure the expected outcome.** The scoring sheet has a place for "the
      alert did not fire", "it fired late", "the runbook did not work" and "nothing was found", and the programme states
      that a drill with no finding must record what was tried that could have produced one. A template that can only
      record success would produce only successes.
- [ ] **Negative:** the procedure forbids the two failure modes `security-operations-targets.md` section 6.3 names —
      repairing before evidence is preserved, and letting a money or custody action through to be reconciled later — and
      the stuck-outbox drill demonstrates the second: an operator replay goes through `admin.outbox.replay` with a
      reason and step-up, and **never** by editing a row.
- [ ] **Exception:** the two game days that cannot be run (provider outage, duplicate callback) are recorded as
      **scheduled, not skipped**, each with the issue that unblocks it and the release gate it must close before. A
      deferral record with all six fields of `docs/process/definition-of-ready.md` section 5 is written for them, and
      **no waiver is written** — `docs/process/waivers.md` is for a gate that cannot be met, not for work whose
      prerequisite has not shipped.
- [ ] The **effectiveness-review** section of `docs/process/game-days.md` states how a scored drill becomes a change to
      the alert rules or the runbooks of children 5 and 5b — who raises it, against which issue, and within what time —
      so the review has a mechanism rather than an intention. Running the review is child 9b's.
