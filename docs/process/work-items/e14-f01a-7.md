Sub-issue of #56. Plan Section 8 assigns #56a `docs/security/vulnerability-management.md` ("triage, SLAs critical 7 /
high 30 / medium 90 days, disclosure") and `docs/security/exceptions.md` ("finding, approver, expiry, compensating

fails on an expired exception", and `docs/nfr/traceability.md` row `NFR-SE-08` is "Proposed, to be confirmed; proof
scheduled (#56a)". On the machine side the acceptance mechanism **already exists and is half the wrong shape**:
`.github/sarif-accepted.json` entries are validated in `scripts/sarif-gate.py` (`load_accepted`) for `scope`, `rule`,
`path`, `message_contains`, `reason` and `reviewed` — **there is no expiry, no owner and no link to a register**, so
an acceptance written once lasts for ever. This sub-issue closes the process gap and that mechanism gap together.

**Read this before touching the register, because the obvious design is wrong.** The `$comment` preamble of
`.github/sarif-accepted.json` already draws the distinction this sub-issue must honour, in its own words: *"This
register is for findings that are wrong. A finding that is right but is being carried anyway is a different thing
and belongs in docs/process/waivers.md, with an owner and an expiry."* The two entries in the file today are of the
first kind — `cs/cleartext-storage-of-sensitive-information` on `SessionRotationReason.PasswordChanged` and
`SessionEndReason.PasswordChanged`, which are enum reason codes and not credentials. **A false positive has no
remediation deadline**, so making `expires` a required field on every entry would put an expiry date on two correct
readings of correct code and turn `main` red on that date for no defect. It would also make the "window must not
exceed the severity's remediation period" rule meaningless, because a finding that is not real has no severity.
The schema below therefore discriminates on `kind` and requires the new fields only where they mean something.

## What is missing

- No triage process: nothing says who rates a finding, against what, within how long, or how a disputed rating is
  settled. `NFR-SE-08` is "Proposed, to be confirmed" with no artefact.
- No disclosure route: nothing tells somebody outside the project where to report a vulnerability, or what happens
  next. There is no `SECURITY.md`.
- No exception register, so the parent's fifth acceptance criterion — "security exceptions are time-bound, approved
  and visible" — is unmeasurable.
- No expiry in the machine register **for an entry that carries a real finding**: nothing distinguishes "this
  finding is wrong" from "this finding is right and we are living with it", so the second cannot be given an
  approver, a deadline or a register identifier at all.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A finding can be taken end to end on paper: somebody outside the project finds the route in `SECURITY.md`,
      reports it, it is triaged and rated against `docs/nfr/security-operations-targets.md` section 2.2, it gets a
      remediation deadline from section 2.1, and either it is fixed and verified in production or it is accepted as a
      `SEC-EX-nnnn` entry with an approver, an expiry and a compensating control.
- [ ] `python3 scripts/sarif-gate.py --self-test` passes and covers, as named sub-checks: a missing or unknown
      `kind` fails; an `accepted-risk` entry missing `exception`, `owner` or `expires` fails; an `accepted-risk`
      entry with a past `expires` fails; an `accepted-risk` entry whose window exceeds its severity's remediation
      period fails; an `accepted-risk` entry naming a `SEC-EX-nnnn` identifier absent from the register fails; a
      `false-positive` entry carrying `expires` fails; a valid `false-positive` entry passes; and a valid in-date
      `accepted-risk` entry passes and is printed with its approver, expiry and days remaining.
- [ ] **The two existing entries stay green and stay permanent.** After migration to `kind: "false-positive"` the
      `codeql` job passes with no expiry on either, and the pull request shows the run. An implementation that puts
      an expiry date on `SessionRotationReason.PasswordChanged` has misread the register and must be changed, not
      waived — the preamble's "findings that are wrong" sentence is the authority.
- [ ] **Negative case, the gate gates**: a deliberately broken branch proves the expiry rule in continuous
      integration, not only in the self-test — one run, with an `accepted-risk` entry whose `expires` is yesterday,
      failing the `codeql` job at the gate step with a readable message. Linked in the pull request, branch deleted
      afterwards. This is the `docs/process/workflow-demo.md` pattern.
- [ ] **Negative case, no silent pass**: an `accepted-risk` entry cannot be made to pass by removing its `expires`
      — that is a missing-required-field failure, not a waiver. The self-test covers it.
- [ ] **Exception case**: the exception path is bounded. An exception cannot be written for longer than its severity's
      remediation window, cannot exist without an approver and a corrective issue, and cannot be renewed by editing
      the expiry date — a renewal is a new identifier with a new approval, and the document says so. The old
      identifier is retired, never reused, matching the identifier discipline of `docs/templates/threat-model.md`.
- [ ] **No invented number**: every service level, severity threshold and waiver duration is cited from
      `docs/nfr/security-operations-targets.md` section 2.1 (critical 7 calendar days, high 30, medium 90, low next
      scheduled release) or `docs/process/release-gates.md` section 4. This sub-issue introduces no figure of its
      own and quotes the existing Trivy thresholds (9.0, 7.0) only where it names them.
- [ ] `docs/process/waivers.md` section 9 and the new register cross-reference each other, and the distinction
      between a waiver and an exception appears in both, in one sentence each, with no contradiction.
- [ ] `./scripts/dev docs` passes, `dotnet format --verify-no-changes` is clean and every test tier that ran before
      still runs. No `.cs` file and no workflow file is changed, so the .NET tiers must be byte-for-byte unaffected.
