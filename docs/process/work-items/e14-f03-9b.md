Sub-issue of #58. **This is not a coding session.** It is the **exercise and evidence** issue: four game days are
*injected, observed, timed and scored* against a live stack, and the alert-and-runbook effectiveness review turns what
they found into changes. Read this before picking it up, because the shape of the work is different from every sibling
under #58:

- it needs a **running Docker daemon** and the `observability` compose profile of child 3 — `./scripts/dev doctor` in
  the planning container reports no Docker daemon, so it cannot be done there at all;
- it needs a **live Alertmanager and Prometheus** with the rules of children 5 and 5b loaded, because the thing being
  measured is whether an alert fires and how fast;
- it needs a **second person** — `docs/nfr/security-operations-targets.md` section 6 and the #58 blueprint both mean a
  drill that is *observed*, and a responder who wrote the runbook is not testing the runbook;
- its primary deliverable is **records, not code**: four game-day records with scoring sheets, and whatever changes to
  children 5 and 5b's rules and runbooks the scores demand.

It is carved out of child 9 for exactly that reason. Child 9 writes the procedure, the templates and the six scripts
from a clean checkout with no environment; this issue executes four of them. Merging the two would have produced either
a blocked pull request or a fabricated drill record, and a fabricated drill record is worse than no drill.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The four drills are **run and recorded**, each naming: the fault injected and how, which alert fired, how long
      detection took **against its section 6.2 target**, which runbook was followed, whether it worked unmodified, and
      what changed as a result.
- [ ] Each record names the **observer**, who is not the person who wrote the runbook being tested, and the machine and
      commit the drill ran against.
- [ ] **Negative — a drill that fails is recorded as a failure.** At least one of the four is reported honestly against
      its own criteria, including any alert that did not fire, fired late, or carried a runbook that did not work. **A
      clean sheet across all four is itself a finding that must be explained**: the record states what was tried that
      could have produced a failure and why it did not. This is the criterion that makes the issue worth doing; a drill
      programme that only ever passes is a programme that is not adversarial.
- [ ] **Negative — the two forbidden mitigations are proved unnecessary.** The stuck-outbox drill recovers through
      `admin.outbox.replay` with step-up and a reason and **never** by editing a row; no drill disables the malware
      scanner (**FMD-05**); and no drill repairs before evidence is preserved (section 6.3). A drill that took a
      shortcut is re-run.
- [ ] **Negative — no personal or financial data leaves the exercise.** Every log excerpt, trace excerpt or screenshot
      attached to a record is reviewed against `docs/nfr/data-classification.md` section 8 **before** it is attached —
      no customer name, amount, measurement, token or `/c/**` path — and every example is synthetic. A drill record is
      evidence that will be read by more people than a log is.
- [ ] **Exception — a drill blocked by an unmerged sibling is recorded, not skipped.** If **#58 child 6** has not
      merged, the object-storage drill records that the storage health check does not exist and that the *absence* is
      what was measured, with child 6 named. If **#59** has not merged, the failed-deployment drill records that it
      exercised the manual route of `docs/architecture/deployment.md` instead. Neither is a waiver and neither is a
      pass.
- [ ] The effectiveness review produces a named change with an issue or pull-request number, or a one-line no-change
      statement with the evidence behind it.

## Migration and rollback

No database change in the repository. **Each drill changes state in a running environment**, and this is the one issue
under #58 where that is true, so two rules apply and the record states both for each drill: the fault is injected only
on development or the interim staging, **never production**; and the script's own reversal step is performed and
confirmed before the drill is closed. The failed-deployment drill is the sharpest case — it deliberately breaks a
deployment, so the record names the environment, the rollback that restored it, and the time it took, which is itself
the RTO evidence `docs/nfr/slo.md` section 6 asks for. Rollback of the pull request is reverting the records, which
loses the evidence and nothing else.
