Sub-issue of #60. The destination half of the parent's first two implementation steps, and all of its third:
the bucket that cannot be emptied by whoever breaks in, the object-storage copy of customer media, and the

## Migration and rollback

No database change: no table, column, index or trigger, and no EF Core migration. Proof is
`dotnet run --project src/Tools/Tailor360.Cli -- migrate --dry-run` printing "The database is up to date with this
build." and the `migration-check` job reporting no model change.

The rollback that matters is the bucket's, and it is asymmetric, so `docs/platform/backups.md` must say it plainly:
enabling versioning and object lock is safe to repeat and **cannot be undone** for objects already written under a
retention period; a lifecycle rule can be reverted by replacing it; a wrongly **long** retention costs storage,
while a wrongly short one destroys evidence, so the script's failure mode is always to refuse rather than to relax.
Reverting this pull request leaves the buckets as they are — a revert removes the scripts, not the policies — and
that must be stated, because a reader will otherwise assume a revert restores the previous state.
